## 📶 Lesson (late morning, from my phone at work)

- **Subnet drill /27:** mask 255.255.255.224, block of 32, 30 usable hosts. Typed the broadcast as ".311" (it's .31) — a reminder that an octet never goes past 255, and a typo like that in a config breaks the line.
- **OSI as troubleshooting vocabulary:** layers 1, 2, 3, 4 and 7 (5 and 6 barely show up). Troubleshoot **bottom-up**: "it's a layer 1 issue" means check the port, the cable, the light on the fiber before anything else. TCP/IP's 4 layers map onto OSI's 7.
- **ARP:** the request goes out as a **broadcast** ("who has this IP?"), the reply comes back as **unicast**, and the answer is cached in the ARP table until it expires. That's why the first ping on a Cisco router shows `.!!!!`.
- **Switch MAC table:** the switch learns from the **source** MAC and the port it arrived on; unknown destinations get **flooded** out every other port.

**Open fault to solve next session:** a server is replaced with the same IP; for a few minutes nobody can reach it, then it starts working on its own. Which table holds the stale info?
